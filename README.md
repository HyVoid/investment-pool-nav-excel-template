## The Problem: Why DIY Percentage Spreadsheets Silently Cost You Money

When organizers first pool money for stocks, crypto, real estate, or private deals, they usually track ownership with a simple cash-contributed percentage (`Your Cash / Total Cash`). The moment the portfolio grows or drops and someone adds or withdraws money mid-year, flat percentages break—creating quiet financial transfers between partners.

**The Broken DIY Percentage Workflow (High Dispute & Audit Risk):**

```text
New partner deposits cash mid-year after the portfolio already grew +30%
-> Organizer manually rewrites ownership % formulas in Excel
-> Early investors' prior profits accidentally get diluted by the late deposit
-> Cell references break (#REF! / #VALUE!) when dragging formulas down
-> Partner dispute erupts when someone asks to cash out
```

**The Automated NAV Unitization Workflow (1-Minute Copy-Paste Clarity):**

```text
Paste deposit, withdrawal, and current portfolio balance into the ledger
-> Pre-built formulas automatically calculate today's Share Price (NAV per Unit)
-> Early investors' gains stay 100% locked inside the higher share price
-> New deposits buy units at today's live price—exact buyout balances update instantly
```

### Three Hidden Traps in Manual Pool Spreadsheets

1. **Trap 1: Giving Away Early Profits on Late Deposits (10%–40% Leakage)**
   Suppose you invest **$100,000** and grow the pool to **$130,000** (+$30,000 profit). A friend then joins with **$65,000**, bringing total value to **$195,000**. Under a flat percentage split (`$65,000 / $195,000 = 33.33%`), if you split profits by contributed cash ratio, **you silently hand up to $10,000 of your pre-entry profit to the late entrant**.
2. **Trap 2: The Loss-Subsidy Penalty During Market Dips**
   Suppose a $100,000 pool drops 20% to **$80,000**, and a new partner adds **$40,000** to buy the dip (total value **$120,000**). Dividing by total historic cash (`$40,000 / $140,000 = 28.57%`) values the new partner's account at just **$34,285** (`28.57% x $120,000`)—an instant **$5,715 Day-1 loss** caused purely by bad spreadsheet math.
3. **Trap 3: Fragile `XIRR` Hacks & Expensive Tax-Season Cleanups**
   Excel's `XIRR` function only outputs an annualized percentage return—it cannot calculate each partner's **exact dollar liquidation payout today** or reconcile to your brokerage balance. Reconstructing broken monthly tabs at tax time routinely costs **$2,500+ in external CPA cleanup fees**, while cloud fund software costs **$3,000–$15,000/year** and exposes private partner net worth to third-party servers.

## How "Mutual Fund Unitization" Works (Explained in Plain English)

This template applies the exact **Unitized Net Asset Value (NAV)** method used by Vanguard ETFs, Fidelity mutual funds, and regulated private funds (aligned with [FASB ASC 946](https://www.fasb.org), [IOSCO Valuation Principles](https://www.iosco.org), and [CFA Institute Pooled Vehicle Standards](https://www.cfainstitute.org))—automated so you never do the math by hand:

```text
1. Share Price (NAV)   = Total Pool Market Value / Total Units Already Issued
2. New Units Issued    = New Cash Deposited / Current Share Price (NAV)
3. Partner Cash Value  = Partner's Units Held * Current Share Price (NAV)
4. True Ownership %    = Partner's Units Held / Total Pool Units
```
